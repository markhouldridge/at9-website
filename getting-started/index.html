How At9 works

At9 keeps every booking your business takes in one diary — on your phone, on a tablet, and on a web page your customers can book from.

However you set it up, the shape is the same:

  1. Tell At9 what you offer — your rooms, tables, services, classes or events.
  2. Say when you take bookings — your opening hours, and any times you're closed.
  3. Take bookings — add them yourself, or let customers book from your own web link.

Everything else — customers, payments, reminders, your team — hangs off those three.

New to At9? Work straight through this guide in order using the Next link at the bottom of each page. It follows the same order you'd set your business up in.

The five kinds of booking

At9 handles five kinds of bookable thing. Pick whichever match your business — you can use more than one, and you can add others later.

  • Rooms — hotels, B&Bs, pubs with rooms, catteries and kennels. Booked over nights, with check-in and check-out.
  • Tables — restaurants, cafés and bars. Booked for a sitting, with a number of guests.
  • Services — salons, nail technicians, tutors, therapists. One or more services booked back to back with a member of staff. You'll see these called appointments too.
  • Classes — gyms, yoga and fitness instructors. A repeating session with a set number of places.
  • Events — training courses, workshops, one-off and multi-day events. Places sold per day, optionally with ticket types.

They all behave the same way once booked: the same diary, the same customer records, the same emails, the same cancellations.

💡

You chose one of these when you signed up. That's only the starting point — add any of the others whenever you like.

Your account

Create your account

Open At9 and tap Register on the welcome screen.

  1. Enter your name, email address and a password of at least 8 characters.
  2. Tap Register. At9 creates your account and emails you a verification link.
  3. Open the email and tap the link to confirm your address.
  4. Return to the app and sign in.

At9 then asks whether you run a business or are booking as a customer. Choose I run a business and pick what you take bookings for.

💡

Check your spam folder if the email hasn't arrived within a minute.

Your business

Create your business

The first time you sign in as a business, At9 asks you to set one up.

  1. Enter your business name and a short description.
  2. Choose your web booking name — the short name that appears in your booking link.
  3. Tap Create.

That's your organisation. Everything else — your rooms or tables, your diary, your customers, your team — belongs to it.

Your business

Business details and opening hours

Go to Settings → Organisation.

  1. Edit your name and description — both appear to customers.
  2. Set your operating hours for each day: when you open and when you close.
  3. Tap Save.

Operating hours are the outer limit of your day. Nothing can be booked outside them, by you or by a customer.

💡

Closed on Mondays? Switch the day off entirely rather than setting the same time for open and close.

Your business

Your web booking link

Every business gets its own booking page at at9.app/book/your-name. Customers can book from it without downloading anything.

  1. Go to Settings → Organisation.
  2. Set your web booking name — keep it short and recognisable, like your business name.
  3. Tap Save, and your link appears beneath it.

Put it in your Instagram bio, your Google listing, your email signature, or a sign on the counter.

Changing the name changes the link. Anywhere you've already shared the old one will stop working, so pick a name you're happy to keep.

Your business

Who can find and book you

Two switches in Settings → Organisation control this, and they do different jobs.

  • Visible in public search — whether customers can find you by searching in the At9 app. Turn it off and you're reachable only by your own link.
  • Allow public bookings — whether anyone can book you online at all. Turn it off and your booking link stops taking bookings.

Turning off public bookings never affects you. You can always add bookings yourself — handy if you're fully booked for a season, or taking a break.

Plans and billing

Your free trial

Every new business gets 90 days free from the day you register. No card, nothing to cancel, and full access to everything — including the Team features.

When the trial ends, your bookings, customers and settings all stay exactly where they are. You'll need a plan to keep taking new bookings.

Plans and billing

Solo and Team

Solo is everything you need to run bookings on your own: your diary, customers, availability, email confirmations, your web booking link, and messaging customers.

Team adds what a business with staff needs:

  • Booking reminders — automatic emails the day before, which cut no-shows.
  • Files — photos and documents saved against a customer or a booking.
  • Team access — invite staff to one shared diary.
  • Staff calendars, customer notes and booking history.

To subscribe, open the menu and tap Subscriptions, then choose a plan. Payment goes through the App Store or Google Play using the card already on your device.

💡

Start on Solo if you're not sure. You can move up to Team the day you need it.

Plans and billing

Change or cancel your plan

Open the menu and tap Subscriptions to see what you're on and switch between Solo and Team.

Because plans are bought through the App Store or Google Play, cancelling is done there — in your Apple or Google account settings, not in At9. There's no minimum term and no cancellation fee.

Cancelling stops future charges. Your data stays yours — email contact@at9.app if you'd like a copy of it.

What you offer

Adding and editing what you offer

Everything you take bookings for is managed the same way, whichever kind it is.

  1. From the home screen, tap Manage next to the type you want — or open Schedule and use the menu in the header to switch type.
  2. Tap Add to create one, or tap an existing one to edit it.
  3. Fill in the Details tab, then the Payment tab if you take money up front.
  4. Tap Save.

You can add as many as you like, and reorder them by dragging so the ones you use most sit at the top.

💡

Only administrators can add or change what you offer. Everyone else sees the diary and takes bookings.

What you offer

Rooms

For hotels, B&Bs, pubs with rooms, catteries and kennels — anything booked overnight.

Give each room a name, how many it sleeps, and a price per night. Set your check-in and check-out times once in Settings → Rooms and every room uses them.

Rooms are booked as a date range. At9 won't let the same room be booked twice over the same nights, however the booking arrives.

What you offer

Tables

For restaurants, cafés, bars and pubs.

Give each table a name and how many it seats. In Settings → Tables, set how long a sitting lasts and how long you need between them to turn the table around.

When a customer books for four, At9 only offers tables that seat four.

What you offer

Services and appointments

For salons, nail technicians, tutors, therapists — anything booked as an appointment with a person.

Give each service a title, how long it takes, and a price. Then choose which members of staff provide it.

A customer can book several services back to back — a cut and a colour become one 90-minute appointment with one person, not two separate bookings. At9 works out the slots from your staff's working hours and everything already in their diary.

A service with nobody assigned to it can't be booked, and won't be shown to customers.

What you offer

Classes

For gyms, yoga and fitness instructors — a session that repeats with a set number of places.

Give each class a title, the days and times it runs, and how many places there are. Add a price if you charge for it.

At9 counts places for you and stops taking bookings when a class is full. You can mark someone as a no-show afterwards.

Events

For training courses, workshops and one-off or multi-day events.

Give each event a title, its dates, and how many places there are. A multi-day event sells places per day, so someone can come to just the Tuesday.

You can add ticket types — standard and concession, say — each with its own price and number of places.

Taking payments

How payments work

At9 takes card payments through Stripe. Money goes straight to your own Stripe account — it never passes through At9, and payouts land in your bank on Stripe's normal schedule.

You're the merchant: your business name appears on the customer's statement, and refunds and disputes are yours to handle.

There are three things to set up, in order: connect Stripe, decide what each thing costs, then choose whether you take the money up front or on arrival.

Previous← Events NextConnect Stripe →
Taking payments

Connect Stripe

Go to Settings → Payments and tap Connect Stripe.

  1. Stripe opens and asks for your business and bank details.
  2. Work through the form — Stripe handles the checks.
  3. You're returned to At9, and the screen shows your status.

Until Stripe says you're ready to take payments, At9 won't ask customers for money. If Stripe needs anything more from you, it's listed on this screen.

Already have a Stripe account? Sign in with it during setup and it'll be connected.

Previous← How payments work NextDeposits and full payment →
Taking payments

Deposits and full payment

Open anything you offer and go to its Payment tab. Choose one of three:

  • No payment — book now, pay however you normally do.
  • Deposit — a fixed amount to hold the booking, with the rest due later.
  • Full amount — the whole price.

Then choose when you take it: up front, or on arrival. Up front means the booking isn't confirmed until the card goes through — which is what stops no-shows on a busy Saturday.

Card payments have a minimum of 30p. Anything smaller is treated as no payment.

Taking payments

Take a payment in the app

When a customer owes you — a balance after a deposit, or a booking set to pay on arrival — you can take it on your phone.

  1. Open the booking from your diary.
  2. Tap Take payment.
  3. Enter the customer's card details and confirm.

The booking updates to paid straight away, and the amount outstanding drops to zero.

Taking payments

Refund a booking

Cancel the booking first, then open it and tap Refund. You can refund the full amount or part of it.

The refund goes back to the card the customer paid with. It usually reaches them in five to ten days, depending on their bank.

The schedule

  • Use the menu in the header to switch between rooms, tables, services, classes and events.
  • Tap any booking to open it.

Add a booking

Tap Add booking from the schedule, or the + button.

  1. Choose what's being booked.
  2. Pick the date and time — At9 only offers what's actually free.
  3. Choose an existing customer or add a new one there and then.

Cancelled bookings aren't deleted — they stay in your booking history so you keep the full record.

When you take bookings

The four kinds of availability

Four settings decide when something can be booked. They stack, from the widest to the most specific:

  1. Operating hours — when your business is open at all.
  2. Bookable times — when each kind of thing can be booked, within those hours.
  3. Closed periods — whole days you're shut, like Christmas or a holiday.
  4. Blocked slots — a recurring gap, like a lunch break.

On top of those, each member of staff has their own working hours.

💡

If a slot isn't being offered when you expect it, work down this list in order — it's almost always one of these four.

When you take bookings

Operating hours

Go to Settings → Organisation and set when you open and close each day. Switch off any day you don't trade.

Nothing can be booked outside these hours, so they're the first thing to get right.

When you take bookings

Bookable times

Go to Settings → Bookable times to set when each kind of thing can be booked — separately for rooms, tables, services, classes and events.

This is how a restaurant open all day takes table bookings only between 12–2 and 6–10.

When you take bookings

Closed periods

Go to Settings → Closed periods and add a date range you're shut — a bank holiday, a refurbishment, two weeks in August.

Nothing can be booked across those dates by anyone, and they're hidden from your booking link.

Previous← Bookable times NextBlocked slots →
When you take bookings

Blocked slots

Go to Settings → Blocked slots to block out a recurring part of the day — a lunch break, a standing delivery, cleaning between sittings.

Unlike a closed period, this repeats weekly on the days you choose.

When you take bookings

Your own working hours

Go to Settings → My working hours to set the days and times you personally work, plus any leave.

This matters most for appointments: At9 only offers a customer a slot when the person providing that service is actually working and free.

Everyone on your team sets their own.

What's on a customer's record

Open a customer and you'll see:

  • Their contact details, which you can edit.
  • Their bookings — past and upcoming.

Bookings from the web

How customers book online

Your booking link — at9.app/book/your-name — works in any browser, with nothing to download and no account needed.

A customer picks what they want, chooses a date and time from what's genuinely free, enters their name and email, and pays if you ask for payment. They get a confirmation email with a reference straight away, and the booking appears in your diary immediately.

Everything you've set up is respected: your opening hours, bookable times, closed periods, and how many places are left.

Previous← Remove a customer NextWhat customers are sent →
Bookings from the web

What customers are sent

At9 emails your customers on your behalf. Replies come back to your business address.

  • Confirmation — as soon as they book.
  • Updated — if the date, time or details change.
  • Cancelled — if the booking is cancelled, with your reason if you gave one.
  • Reminder — the day before. Team plan.
  • Waiting list offer — when a place comes up.
Previous← How customers book online NextWaiting lists →
Bookings from the web

Waiting lists

When a class or event is full, customers can join the waiting list instead of going elsewhere.

If someone cancels, At9 offers the place to the first person waiting and emails them. They get a set time to accept before it passes to the next person.

You don't have to do anything — it runs on its own.

Your team

Invite someone to your team

Go to Settings → People and tap Invite.

  1. Ask them to install At9 and register first.
  2. Enter the email address they registered with.
  3. Send the invitation. It appears in their app for them to accept.

Once they've accepted, they share your diary and your customers.

Team access is part of the Team plan, and included in your free trial.

Previous← Waiting lists NextWhat your team can do →
Your team

What your team can do

There are two levels.

  • Administrators can change anything — what you offer, your hours, payments, and who's on the team.
  • Everyone else works the diary: taking, changing and cancelling bookings, and managing customers.

Front-of-house can run the day without being able to change your prices or your opening hours.

Each person sets their own working hours, so appointments are only offered when they're actually in.

Previous← Invite someone to your team NextRemove someone →
Your team

Remove someone

Go to Settings → People, open the person, and turn them off or remove them.

They lose access straight away. Bookings they took stay exactly where they are.

You can't remove the last administrator — someone has to be able to manage the business.
