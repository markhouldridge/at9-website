01

The colour triad

Primary · Violet

Confident, not corporate

Deep violet anchors every action. CTAs, the logo, navigation — this is the colour users remember and trust. It signals craft and capability without tipping into cold enterprise blue.

Secondary · Fuchsia-Pink

Warm and human

Fuchsia-pink adds the human touch — empty states, selected card borders, 'Current Plan' badges. Cool enough (magenta direction) to read as pink, not red. At least 47° of hue distance from error keeps the two unmistakably separate.

Tertiary · Lime

Alive in the details

Lime earns attention by staying rare. One dot, one tick, one badge. Never a background. Use it and the eye finds it; overuse it and it becomes noise.

12

Design rules — Do / Don't

Team Plan
Selected — 2px secondary border
Do ✓ — Secondary for selection
Use secondary (pink) for selected card states. Primary is for actions. Secondary marks state.
Team Plan
Selected — 2px primary border
Don't ✗ — Primary for selection
Avoid using primary for selection state — it creates visual competition with primary CTA buttons on the same screen.
Do ✓ — Error outlined for destructive
Outlined error stays calm. It communicates danger without dominating. The user still sees it.
Don't ✗ — Lime as button fill
Don't use tertiary at scale. Its value is scarcity — a small badge or dot. Fill a button with it and you've lost the signal.

Customers manage their own bookings.
View, track, and cancel — directly from the at9 app. Your support queue gets shorter. You focus on the people in front of you.

Overnight room stays and same-day table sittings, side by side. Drag to reschedule, tap to view — nothing falls through the cracks.

Built-in messaging
Talk to customers without switching apps.
Every conversation is linked to its booking, so context is never lost. No more WhatsApp threads tangled with personal messages.
🕐
Operating hours
Set your hours once. Never explain again.
Configure opening and closing times and at9 enforces them automatically. Customers only ever see availability inside your windows.
🚫
Date blocking
Close the calendar in seconds.
Block any date or range — public holidays, maintenance, staff days. Customers see you as unavailable. No back-and-forth needed.
⚡
Bookable slots
Granular control over when you're open.
Fine-tune available booking windows beyond just your opening hours. Different rules for rooms and tables? at9 handles both independently.
📝
Booking notes
Details that don't get forgotten.
Add private notes to any booking — dietary needs, preferences, special requests. They travel with the reservation, not stuck in someone's head.
